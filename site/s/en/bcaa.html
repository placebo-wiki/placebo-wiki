BCAA: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. BCAA may help only people with cirrhosis and hepatic encephalopathy; for sport and muscles, with enough protein in the food, there is no effect.

it depends an extract of 5 766 studies

What research shows

BCAA may reduce the symptoms of hepatic encephalopathy, but only in people with cirrhosis: in a Cochrane review this was tested on 827 people, and the supplement does not affect mortality.

What is not proven

It is unknown whether BCAA help muscles and sport: with enough protein in the food there is no effect.

Myths

The myth "BCAA build muscles without food" is wrong: with enough protein there is no effect.

The myth "BCAA are good for the liver for everyone" is wrong: the effect exists only in encephalopathy with cirrhosis, and the supplement does not affect mortality.

How to take it

Dose from the fact sheet: 5-10 g around the workout. On the timing of the effect: in encephalopathy the intake is symptomatic, in courses, and for muscles no quick effect is established. No formal upper limit has been set: there are no data on toxicity.

Who should be careful

The supplement makes sense only in cirrhosis with hepatic encephalopathy, so healthy people and athletes without a protein shortage should not take it: plain protein or a protein powder works better.

With medicines

Levodopa: BCAA compete with this medicine for absorption.
