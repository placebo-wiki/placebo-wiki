Resveratrol: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. Resveratrol is a polyphenol of grapes. It may help only in type 2 diabetes and with excess weight, and for healthy people the effect is not proven.

it depends an extract of 817 studies

What research shows

Resveratrol may help lower fasting glucose, HbA1c and insulin resistance, but only in type 2 diabetes.

In type 2 diabetes resveratrol probably lowers blood pressure and improves vascular function (FMD +1.43%).

What is not proven

In humans the bioavailability of resveratrol is close to zero, so it is unknown whether it helps in usual supplement doses: in humans the effect is not proven.

For cardiovascular outcomes the effect of resveratrol is not proven.

Myths

The myth "like a glass of wine": the French paradox is marketing, the doses of resveratrol in wine are tiny.

The myth "anti-aging": in humans bioavailability is close to zero, and usual supplements are far from the doses in clinical studies.

How to take it

Dose from trials: 100-500 mg. The effects work by courses, they appear over weeks or months, there is no fast action. No formal upper limit is set: there are no data on toxicity.

Who should be careful

The clinical value of resveratrol is often not confirmed, and bioavailability is low, so healthy people most likely do not need it.

With medicines

Anticoagulants: resveratrol strengthens their effect, so before joint intake a doctor's consultation is needed.

NSAIDs: resveratrol gives an extra antiplatelet effect, which raises the risk of bleeding.
