Selenium: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. Selenium probably lowers antibodies and TSH, but only in patients with Hashimoto's thyroiditis, and for healthy people with a normal level the supplement is not indicated.

it depends an extract of 1 429 studies

What research shows

In patients with Hashimoto's thyroiditis selenium probably lowers the levels of the antibodies TPOAb and TgAb and TSH, a 2024 meta-analysis showed this.

Selenium probably lowers MDA when taken as a supplement, a 2024 meta-analysis showed this.

What is not proven

Selenium may help, but only with Hashimoto's or a deficiency: in healthy people with a normal level there is no effect.

Myths

The myth "selenium is good for the thyroid for everyone" is wrong: the effect has been shown in Hashimoto's, not in everyone.

The myth "worth taking just in case" is wrong: with a normal selenium level no benefit has been shown.

The myth "any form is the same" is wrong: the forms of selenium were not compared in the reviews.

How to take it

The exact dose of selenium in supplements is not given in the reviews, agree the dose with a doctor. It is worth taking only with a confirmed deficiency or Hashimoto's disease; with a normal status the supplement is not indicated. In Hashimoto's the lowering of antibodies and TSH was described over a course of intake, the exact timing is not singled out in the meta-analysis; the duration is determined by the dynamics of antibodies and TSH under a doctor's control. No upper limit has been set: high doses give a risk of selenosis.

Who should be careful

Healthy people with a normal selenium level do not need the supplement.

Everyone should keep in mind that selenium is toxic in excess: selenosis is possible, so the dose needs to be agreed with a doctor.

With medicines

Statins: selenium may reduce their effect, though these data are disputed.
