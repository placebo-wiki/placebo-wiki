L-carnitine: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends (evidence level C). L-carnitine carries fatty acids into the mitochondria, but the effects are modest: about 1 kg of weight and a small shift of markers in type 2 diabetes, and for weight loss in healthy people it helps poorly.

What research shows

L-carnitine may help in diabetic polyneuropathy, but only as an addition to the main therapy: the clinical benefit is not proven.

L-carnitine may help increase walking distance in intermittent claudication, but only a little: according to a Cochrane review of 2021 the effect is not significant.

What is not proven

It is unknown whether L-carnitine lowers weight: the effect is minimal, about 1 kg, and the benefit for recovery has not been proven in humans.

It is unknown whether L-carnitine helps in type 2 diabetes: in studies HbA1c fell by 0.16%, BMI by 0.37 and LDL by 0.11; the effect is very small.

Myths

The myth "L-carnitine melts fat in the mitochondria" is wrong: the claimed effect is about 1 kg of weight and contradictory.

The myth "this is vitamin therapy" is wrong: the ALC and PLC forms studied for neuropathy and claudication are medicinal forms, not supplements.

How to take it

Dose from trials: 1-2 g per day; for metabolism plain L-carnitine fits, and in neuropathy acetyl-L-carnitine is used with doses monitored by a doctor. No instant effect should be expected: in neuropathy courses last 6-12 months, and the shift of markers in type 2 diabetes is assessed after 12-24 weeks. No formal upper limit has been set: there are no data on toxicity.

Who should be careful

People with diabetic neuropathy should not expect benefit from small doses: the effect of acetyl-L-carnitine was noted only at a dose above 1500 mg per day, and the reliability of the data is very low.

With medicines

Warfarin: L-carnitine may strengthen its effect, so the doctor must be told about the supplement.

Thyroid medicines: L-carnitine may lower their effect, so the combination should be discussed with a doctor.
