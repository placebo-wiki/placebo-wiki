DHA: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends, rating C. It is unknown whether DHA helps slow cognitive decline in Alzheimer's disease, and in healthy people the effect is not shown.

it depends an extract of 1 367 studies

What research shows

DHA probably lowers inflammation in neurodegenerative diseases.

DHA may help as a protective factor, but only in nursing mothers: a high concentration of DHA in milk was classed among protective factors.

What is not proven

It is unknown whether DHA helps slow cognitive decline in Alzheimer's disease: on the CDR scale progression was slower, but on the MMSE and ADAS-cog scales there is no difference.

Myths

The myth "omega-3 protects the brain of everyone" is wrong: in neurodegeneration supplements are less effective.

The myth "improves memory in the healthy" is wrong: in the abstracts of studies this is not confirmed.

The myth "the more, the better" is wrong: most often side effects from the stomach and intestines were noted.

How to take it

An exact dose cannot be named: in the abstracts of studies doses are not given; different combinations of EPA, DHA and medical drugs with them were used. The intake is long: in Alzheimer's disease as part of therapy, in nursing mothers for the whole period of breastfeeding, while on the MMSE and ADAS-cog scales no significant changes were recorded. The upper limit is not set in the abstracts.

Who should be careful

The most frequent side effects: from the stomach and intestines, so people with sensitive digestion should be more careful.

DHA does not replace the main therapy: patients with Alzheimer's disease can consider it only as an addition agreed with a doctor.

With medicines

There are no known interactions with medicines, no data on this were found.
