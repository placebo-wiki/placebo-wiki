Calcium: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. In pregnancy calcium works: it lowers the risk of preeclampsia, especially with low calcium in the food. Outside pregnancy, for fractures in older people, meta-analyses contradict each other: some find a benefit together with vitamin D, while fresh reviews find no effect in the general population.

it depends an extract of 9 867 studies

What research shows

In pregnant women calcium helps: blood pressure rises less often (RR 0.65), and the risk of preeclampsia is lower at doses from 1 gram per day, especially with low calcium in the food.

Calcium may help against fractures, but only in people 50+, and especially together with vitamin D. Meta-analyses contradict each other: some find a lower risk, while fresh reviews find no effect in the general population.

What is not proven

It is unknown whether calcium lowers blood pressure in people with normal pressure: the shifts in pressure are small, and the preventive benefit is not established.

Myths

The myth "the more, the better" is wrong: for bone 500-600 mg per day is enough, more does not mean better, and on fractures the reviews are also contradictory.

The myth "it lowers blood pressure for everyone" is wrong: in people with normal pressure the effect is small.

How to take it

First calcium from food: milk, sesame. If a supplement is needed: for bone 500-600 mg per day together with vitamin D, no more; carbonate or citrate is chosen by tolerability. The effect on bone is expected in months and years, and only together with vitamin D. The dose against preeclampsia is different: the effect was studied at intake from 1 gram per day, and in pregnancy it is taken in courses. Upper limit per day: 2500 milligrams; it must not be exceeded.

Who should be careful

Pregnant women: at high doses the risk of HELLP syndrome is raised (RR 2.67), though in absolute terms this is a rare condition.

People 50+ for protection from fractures: meta-analyses contradict each other, and where an effect is found, it comes together with vitamin D.

With medicines

Antibiotics (tetracyclines, quinolones): calcium lowers their absorption; take separately with an interval of 2-4 hours.

Bisphosphonates: calcium and these medicines are taken separately, with an interval of 2 hours.

Iron supplements: they compete with calcium for absorption, so the intakes are spaced in time.
