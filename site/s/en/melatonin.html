Melatonin: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: works. Melatonin speeds up falling asleep. The main condition: it is the hormone of the circadian "biological night" signal, not a sleeping pill, so it is needed for jet lag, shift work, a late sleep type, and at an age when your own synthesis drops.

works an extract of 2 164 studies

What research shows

Melatonin speeds up falling asleep.

Melatonin helps improve sleep quality on the PSQI scale in people with impaired excretion of their own melatonin.

What is not proven

Melatonin may help postpone the need for an analgesic and raise embryo quality, but only in certain situations and not in everyone.

Myths

The myth "more means you fall asleep faster" is wrong: after 1-3 mg the dose-effect curve is flat, and raising the dose does not speed up falling asleep.

The myth "you can take it for months" is wrong: data on long-term use are few, so it is better to take it in courses, as needed.

How to take it

Dose from trials: 0.5-3 mg 30-60 minutes before sleep. The regular form fits for falling asleep; the controlled-release form is for maintaining sleep. Falling asleep speeds up 30-60 minutes after intake, while shifting the rhythm takes 3-5 days of use on a schedule. There is no formal upper limit; the working doses are 0.5-3 mg, and higher doses give side effects, not an effect. For older people the optimal dose is 1-3 mg; megadoses are not needed. Course: as needed.

Who should be careful

People with autoimmune diseases and in pregnancy need to be careful.

Possible side effects: sleepiness and dizziness, so care is needed by those for whom they are unacceptable.

With medicines

Warfarin: melatonin strengthens its effect, so INR monitoring is needed.

SSRI antidepressants: serotonin syndrome is rare, but possible.

Alcohol: it cuts the effect of melatonin and strengthens sedation.
