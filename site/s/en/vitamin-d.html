Vitamin D: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. Vitamin D cuts the number of fractures in older people, especially together with calcium, and it may help mood and thinking, but only with a deficiency. Much depends on the starting level of 25(OH)D: above 50 nmol/L the benefit is almost zero, so a blood test is needed before use. Who is at risk of deficiency: northern latitudes from October to March, office work, dark skin, obesity, age 65+.

What research shows

In older people vitamin D cuts the number of fractures, especially when it is taken together with calcium. Calcium, vitamin D and their combination for the prevention of fractures and falls were tested in a 2026 meta-analysis.

Vitamin D may help mood and thinking, but only with a deficiency.

What is not proven

It is unknown whether vitamin D helps mood and thinking in people without a deficiency: the effect was seen only with a deficiency.

With a starting level of 25(OH)D above 50 nmol/L the benefit of the supplement is almost zero: it is considered meaningful when the level is below 30 nmol/L.

The data on the benefit of the pair D3 + K2 are weaker than the data on the benefit of D3 itself.

Myths

The myth "the more, the better" is wrong: vitamin D is fat-soluble and builds up in the body; a higher dose does not mean more benefit.

The myth "summer sun is enough for the winter" is wrong: the summer store lasts only 2-3 months.

How to take it

Dose: 1000-2000 IU per day. Forms: capsules, including the D3 + K2 variant; D3 (cholecalciferol) raises the level better than D2, and K2 directs calcium into the bones. The effect is not felt subjectively: the level is checked with a 25(OH)D test after 8-12 weeks. In winter it is taken continuously. Upper limit: 4000 international units per day (EFSA); long-term use from 10000 international units and above creates a risk of hypercalcemia.

Who should be careful

Those who have not yet done the test: before taking vitamin D it is a must, because the effect depends on the starting level of 25(OH)D.

Those who take more than 4000 international units per day for a long time: risk of hypercalcemia and stones.

With medicines

Thiazide diuretics: together with vitamin D the risk of hypercalcemia grows; use is discussed with a doctor.

Digoxin: hypercalcemia, which vitamin D can cause at high doses, strengthens the toxicity of digoxin; use is agreed with a doctor.
