Tyrosine: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: unproven, evidence level D. Tyrosine may help cognitive work, but only under acute stress or sleep loss.

unproven an extract of 3 023 studies

What research shows

Tyrosine may help keep working memory and information processing, but only under stress and overload.

What is not proven

In humans it is not proven that tyrosine raises sports performance: there is no effect in most studies.

Myths

The myth "tyrosine speeds up the brain for everyone" is wrong: it may help only against a background of depleted catecholamines, that is under acute stress.

The myth "tyrosine is sport doping" is wrong: performance does not grow.

How to take it

Dose from the fact sheet: 500-2000 mg acutely, once before the load (a competition, a night without sleep), situationally and not for constant intake. The action is acute and one-time; courses have not been studied. No formal upper limit is set: there are no data on toxicity.

Who should be careful

Healthy people do not need tyrosine "for tone": it may help only under acute cognitive loads, sleep loss and high cognitive stress.

The data are few: for cognitive functions under stress there are only single studies of low quality, so in pregnancy, illness and doubt it is better to talk to a doctor first.

With medicines

Tyrosine must not be combined with MAO inhibitors: a hypertensive crisis is possible, this is dangerous.

With levodopa tyrosine competes for absorption, so joint intake is discussed with a doctor.
