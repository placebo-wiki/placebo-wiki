Niacinamide: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. Niacinamide: it depends on the goal. It probably lowers the risk of non-melanoma skin cancer, but only in people who have already had skin cancer; in healthy people the effect is not described.

it depends an extract of 1 684 studies

What research shows

Niacinamide probably lowers the risk of non-melanoma skin cancer, but only in people with such cancer in their history: in a 2022 meta-analysis the relative risk was 0.50.

Niacinamide probably lightens pigment spots with external use: the quality of the studies is high.

What is not proven

It is unknown whether niacinamide helps reduce pigmentation on a par with lasers and other means: this is not confirmed.

Myths

The myth "the lipid data are about niacinamide": they do not relate to nicotinamide.

The myth "nicotinamide is harmless": in a 2022 meta-analysis unwanted effects from the stomach and intestines were noted.

The myth "everyone needs it for skin": the benefit is shown only for groups at risk of skin cancer and with hyperpigmentation.

How to take it

The exact dose for the prevention of skin cancer is not given in the available reviews; externally it is used by the schemes of products for post-inflammatory pigmentation. By mouth it is taken only as prescribed by a doctor with skin cancer in the history. Prevention is a course: the effect on skin cancer and actinic keratoses remains while the intake goes on, unwanted effects from the stomach and intestines were noted. Do not confuse it with niacin, which causes flushing. The upper limit is not set.

Who should be careful

People with a sensitive stomach should take into account: in a 2022 meta-analysis a higher risk of symptoms from the stomach and intestines was noted; external forms are tolerated better.

Healthy people without skin cancer in the history and without pigmentation do not need niacinamide: the effect in the healthy is not described.

With medicines

There are no data on the interaction of niacinamide with medicines: no known interactions are described; with constant intake of drugs it is worth asking a doctor.
