Whey protein: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: works (evidence level A). It helps build muscle and prevent sarcopenia, but the main condition: combination with strength training.

works an extract of 1 363 studies

What research shows

Whey protein helps build muscle mass and prevent sarcopenia: the effect is confirmed by extensive meta-analyses.

In sarcopenia in older people protein raises muscle mass (SMD 0.47) and walking speed (SMD 1.13), and together with strength training it raises grip strength.

With excess weight the effect is strongest in a pair with strength load and a calorie deficit: this was shown by 35 RCTs.

By laboratory markers protein may help, but only in sarcopenia: IL-6 dropped, while IGF-1 and albumin rose.

Myths

The myth "protein builds muscles on its own" is wrong: without strength load there is no effect on sarcopenia, this was shown by 10 RCTs with 1154 participants. The myth "the more the better" is also wrong: above 2.2 grams per kilogram of body weight there is no benefit.

How to take it

A portion of 20-40 g after a workout; per day in total: 1.6-2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight. The effect builds up with training and is seen at 8-16 weeks, it does not give a fast gain in mass. A formal upper limit is not set: there are no toxicity data. With lactose intolerance an isolate is chosen.

Who should be careful

People with lactose intolerance: a usual concentrate may not suit, for such cases an isolate is recommended.

With medicines

Levodopa: protein lowers its absorption, so the drug and the protein are taken separately.
