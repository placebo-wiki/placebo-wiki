Glutamine: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. Glutamine may be needed only in the clinic in critical conditions and by a doctor's prescription; for healthy people the supplement is not indicated, there is no benefit for them.

it depends an extract of 1 095 studies

What research shows

Glutamine may help, but only in intensive care, and even there the data are contradictory: early RCTs showed fewer infections, while 2 large RCTs did not confirm the benefit (Cochrane 2014).

What is not proven

It is unknown whether glutamine helps muscles: the effect is not confirmed, and the benefit for the gut and immunity is discussed only in the clinic.

In humans it is not proven that glutamine helps preterm infants: no difference was found in infections and weight gain (Cochrane 2001).

Myths

The myth: since glutamine is conditionally essential, everyone needs it. In fact in the clinic its benefit is debated (a 2024 meta-analysis). The myth: glutamine for muscles: such RCTs were simply not done.

How to take it

Dose in studies: 5-10 g per day. A fast effect should not be expected: in the clinic we are talking about days and weeks, and the benefit was not confirmed in large RCTs. A formal upper limit is not set: there are no toxicity data. For healthy people with a full diet the supplement is not needed; in the clinic medicines prescribed by a doctor are used, not supplements.

Who should be careful

For healthy people glutamine is not indicated: no benefit outside the clinic was found, the effect is doubtful outside intensive care.

With medicines

Chemotherapy: glutamine may lower its toxicity as an adjuvant.
