Glucosamine + chondroitin: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. In knee osteoarthritis the combination may give modest pain relief in the first months, but with advanced wear and obesity it does not replace treatment.

it depends an extract of 783 studies

What is not proven

It is unknown whether the combination of glucosamine and chondroitin reduces pain in osteoarthritis: the effect is close to placebo.

It is unknown whether the relief lasts long: pain in the first 6 months may be lower by 10%, but long-term effects are not confirmed (43 RCTs).

It is unknown whether the combination improves the state of the joint: on the WOMAC scale there is no significant effect, and the pain reduction on the VAS scale is marginal.

Myths

The myth "it restores cartilage" is wrong: cartilage regeneration has not been shown in RCTs.

The myth "it works like ibuprofen" is wrong: the effect is small and close to placebo, and by a year it fades away.

How to take it

Dose: 1500+1200 mg per day (glucosamine and chondroitin), a course of 3-6 months. The form matters: glucosamine is taken as sulfate, not hydrochloride; if there is no effect, the intake should be stopped. The effect comes with a course: pain may be lower in the first 6 months, and by a year the effect fades away. No formal upper limit has been set: there are no data on toxicity.

Who should be careful

Side effects are rare: serious adverse events with the combination are fewer than with placebo.

Chondroitin works better than glucosamine, and glucosamine sulfate is better than hydrochloride, but the quality of the RCTs is mostly low.

With medicines

Warfarin: the combination may strengthen its effect, so INR monitoring is needed.

Glucose-lowering drugs: the combination may affect blood glucose levels.
