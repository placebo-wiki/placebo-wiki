Probiotics: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. There is no "probiotic in general": the effect is strain-specific, so the strain is chosen for a specific task, for example diarrhea during antibiotics.

it depends an extract of 8 068 studies

What research shows

Probiotics probably help the gut and the immune system, but only for specific indications and only with specific strains.

In children on antibiotics, diarrhea with probiotics probably happens less often: 33 RCTs on 6352 children showed this.

Certain strains probably help prevent Clostridioides difficile-associated diarrhea after antibiotics in adults and children.

According to 82 RCTs, probiotics probably lower the risk of diarrhea during antibiotics: relative risk 0.58, and the result is stable in subgroup analyses.

Myths

The myth "the more billions of CFU, the better": in fact the specific strain and the evidence for exactly that strain matter more.

The myth "a probiotic colonizes the gut for good": by nature it is transitory, that is, it lives in the gut only temporarily.

How to take it

Dose: 1-10 billion CFU; the strain is chosen for the task. For diarrhea during antibiotics, intake starts on day 1-2 together with the antibiotic; for irritable bowel syndrome the effect is assessed after 4-8 weeks. Course: from 4 weeks. There is no formal upper limit, but care is needed with immunosuppression and central venous catheters.

Who should be careful

Probiotics are contraindicated in premature infants: there is an FDA warning from 2023.

They are also contraindicated in patients with a central venous catheter, people with weakened immunity and in acute pancreatitis: there is a risk of bacteremia.

With medicines

Antibiotics: intake is spaced by 2 hours, and the probiotic is continued after the course.

Immunosuppressants: a theoretical risk of infection, so the combination is discussed with a doctor.
