CoQ10: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. Coenzyme Q10 may help, but only in older people and in those taking statins; in healthy people the effect is weak.

it depends an extract of 966 studies

What research shows

Coenzyme Q10 may help with energy, but only in older people and in those taking statins, in whom the CoQ10 level falls; in healthy people the effect is weak.

A deficiency of ubiquinone is linked with the severity of heart failure: with a real lack of the substance the supplement may help, but only people with such a deficiency.

In heart failure coenzyme Q10 may lower mortality and the number of hospitalizations, but the evidence is of moderate quality. The Cochrane review of 2021 finds a relative risk (RR) of 0.58 for mortality and 0.62 for hospitalizations, while the authors see no convincing evidence for routine use.

What is not proven

It is unknown whether coenzyme Q10 lowers blood pressure in primary hypertension: the Cochrane review considers the effect unreliable.

Myths

The myth "coenzyme Q10 gives energy to everyone": in healthy people there is no effect.

The myth "lowers blood pressure": the Cochrane review considers the effect unreliable.

How to take it

Dose: 100-200 mg per day. Forms: ubiquinone and ubiquinol, ubiquinol is more bioavailable but more expensive. The effect is delayed, it is taken in courses, no quick surge of energy is worth waiting for. No formal upper limit is set: there are no data on toxicity.

Who should be careful

People with statin myopathy: the data on the benefit of coenzyme Q10 are contradictory.

People with migraine: there are few data on the benefit.

People with hypertension: the data are few.

With medicines

Warfarin: coenzyme Q10 may lower its effect, INR monitoring is needed.

Antihypertensive drugs: coenzyme Q10 may strengthen their effect.
