Zinc: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. Zinc may help with cognition and testosterone, but only with a deficiency, which is more often found in vegetarians, vegans and people with little animal protein in the diet.

it depends an extract of 3 274 studies

What research shows

Zinc probably lowers menstrual pain in primary dysmenorrhea, this is shown by a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Zinc may help cognitive functions, but only with a zinc deficiency.

Zinc may help raise testosterone, but only with a zinc deficiency, this is confirmed by a systematic review of the link between blood zinc and testosterone.

What is not proven

It is unknown whether zinc lozenges make a cold shorter and milder: there is no reliable confirmation of this.

Myths

The myth "a cold will pass in a day thanks to zinc" is wrong: lozenges only slightly shorten the length of a cold, and even that only if the intake starts in the first 24 hours, in the form of lozenges or syrup and at a dose of 75-100 milligrams per day.

The myth "the more zinc, the more testosterone" is wrong: testosterone grows only with a zinc deficiency.

How to take it

Dose from the fact sheet: 15-30 mg per day, usually in courses of 4-8 weeks. Forms: picolinate, citrate or gluconate, check the amount of elemental zinc on the label. With a deficiency, an effect on taste, skin and healing is expected in 4-8 weeks, while "immunity" is not felt subjectively. Upper limit for long-term intake: 40 milligrams per day, months on doses above 50 milligrams lead to a copper deficiency.

Who should be careful

Those who plan to take zinc long and in high doses: zinc competes with copper, and long intake above the upper limit leads to a copper deficiency.

With medicines

Antibiotics (quinolones, tetracyclines): zinc lowers their absorption, so take them separately, with an interval of 2-4 hours.

Copper supplements: long zinc intake lowers the copper level.
