Iodine: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: unproven. An iodine supplement may help, but only with a confirmed deficiency; in healthy people without a deficiency the benefit is not shown, and with thyroid diseases a doctor's control is needed.

unproven an extract of 2 523 studies

What research shows

Iodine may help support the work of the thyroid gland, but only with its deficiency.

Deficiencies of trace elements matter in acute and chronic diseases, according to the ESPEN 2022 recommendations, but this concerns correcting a deficiency, not supplements for healthy people.

What is not proven

In Hashimoto's disease restricting iodine in the diet gave no effect, a systematic review of 2023; it is unknown whether changing iodine intake helps in this disease.

Myths

The myth "more iodine, better thyroid" is wrong: in a 2023 review a diet with restricted iodine showed no effect.

The myth "everyone needs iodine" is wrong: a supplement makes sense only with an established deficiency.

The myth "iodine is harmless" is wrong: an excess of iodine affects the thyroid gland.

How to take it

There is no specific dose from trials: the reviews give no dosages of iodine supplements, the upper limit is not set, the timing of the effect and the length of the course are not described. It is worth taking only with a confirmed deficiency as prescribed by a doctor; the dose and form are chosen according to status; with thyroid diseases a special scheme is needed. Standard form: potassium iodide in capsules, as in iodized salt; in supplements with kelp the iodine dose floats from batch to batch, it is easy to get an excess.

Who should be careful

People with thyroid diseases: intake only under a doctor's control, an excess of iodine can worsen the course of thyroiditis.

For healthy people without iodine deficiency the supplement is not justified.

With medicines

Antithyroid drugs: iodine acts in the opposite way, the effects mutually weaken each other.

Lithium drugs: the combination with iodine raises the risk of hypothyroidism.
