B9: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: works. Folic acid lowers the risk of neural tube defects in the fetus if intake starts 1-3 months before conception.

works an extract of 4 423 studies

What research shows

Folic acid intake around conception lowers the risk of neural tube defects in the fetus: relative risk 0.28.

Folic acid lowers homocysteine.

What is not proven

It is unknown whether folic acid improves birth outcomes: folate and hemoglobin levels are higher and homocysteine is lower, but birth outcomes do not change.

For cognitive function in older people a Cochrane review shows no benefit.

Myths

The myth "all B vitamins are the same" is wrong: only folate taken around conception lowers the risk of neural tube defects, relative risk 0.28.

The myth "folate improves memory in older people" is wrong: a Cochrane review showed no benefit.

How to take it

Dose: 400-800 mcg per day; with the MTHFR polymorphism, methylfolate (5-MTHF) is chosen, the already active form of folic acid. Start 1-3 months before conception and continue in the first trimester; homocysteine falls within weeks. Upper limit: 1000 micrograms of synthetic folic acid per day.

Who should be careful

Older people should be careful: folic acid masks a B12 deficiency.

With medicines

Methotrexate: folic acid is its antagonist and lowers the effect of chemotherapy; the doctor sets the schedule.

Antiepileptic drugs: folate can lower their concentration, so a doctor's monitoring is needed.
