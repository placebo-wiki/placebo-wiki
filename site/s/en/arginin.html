Arginine: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends, evidence level B. For blood pressure and the pump it may help, but only in certain situations, and it works less well than citrulline.

it depends an extract of 1 832 studies

What research shows

Arginine probably lowers the risk of preeclampsia in pregnant women: in a meta-analysis of 2025 (n=2028) the relative risk was 0.52, and for the severe form 0.23, but the certainty of the data is low.

What is not proven

It is unknown whether arginine helps prevent necrotising enterocolitis in premature infants: there are 3 studies (n=285), but the confirmation is not enough yet.

Myths

The myth "a powerful pump" is wrong: the bioavailability of arginine is low, and citrulline works better.

The myth "more is better" is wrong: above 6 g per day arginine hits the stomach and gut.

The myth that arginine removes preeclampsia is wrong: it only probably lowers the risk, but it is not a treatment.

How to take it

Dose from the fact sheet: 3-6 g per day, and side effects from the stomach and gut are frequent. If arginine, then the L form without "technologies"; for preeclampsia the doctor sets the dose and course. The effect on blood pressure is expected within hours to days; in preeclampsia courses last weeks, and in sport the effect is unpredictable. No formal upper limit has been set: there are no data on toxicity.

Who should be careful

For sport arginine is not recommended: for blood flow citrulline works better, and the effect of arginine is unpredictable.

For erectile function and sugar control the data are limited and mixed, so with these conditions the decision on intake is made together with a doctor.

With medicines

With antihypertensive drugs arginine may strengthen the effect, so blood pressure is monitored and the combination is discussed with a doctor.

With nitrates arginine strengthens the widening of blood vessels; joint intake is possible only with a doctor's agreement.
