Vitamin C: does it work, and how to take it

Verdict: it depends. Vitamin C may help the immune system, but only under extreme loads, for example in marathon runners and people in the cold.

it depends an extract of 2 742 studies

What research shows

Vitamin C probably cuts the frequency of colds in half in marathon runners, skiers and soldiers (RR 0.50).

Under extreme loads (marathon, skiing, army), preventive intake cuts illness rates by 50%.

What is not proven

It is unknown whether vitamin C helps against cardiovascular events: large RCTs showed no benefit, and the number of such events does not drop.

In the general population, prevention does not cut the rate of colds (RR 0.96), and intake after symptoms start does not work.

Myths

The myth "vitamin C prevents a cold" is wrong: in the general population this is not so.

The myth "megadoses are safe" is wrong: high doses give gut problems and a risk of stones.

How to take it

Dose: 500-1000 mg per day. There is usually no subjective effect; with a deficiency (bleeding gums, tiredness), refilling takes 1-2 weeks. Upper limit: 2000 milligrams per day; above that, diarrhea and a risk of kidney stones in people prone to them are possible. Regular ascorbic acid is enough: liposomal and esterified forms are an overpayment without strong data.

Who should be careful

People prone to kidney stones should be careful with doses above the upper limit: above 2000 milligrams per day, the risk of oxalate stones grows.

With medicines

Anticoagulants (warfarin): high doses of vitamin C may lower their effect.

Iron medicines: vitamin C raises iron absorption; this is synergy.
